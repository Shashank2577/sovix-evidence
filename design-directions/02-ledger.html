Exhibit A · Critical

Most changes reach production without a review

Of the changes that went into the product through a pull request, how many had at least one colleague formally look at them and record an opinion. Here it is 7.8%. The strongest single piece of evidence: #16210, 1,936 lines merged with no review.

Recommended action — turn on branch protection requiring one approving review. This is a settings change, not a process programme.

Conventions & caveats

fired: review_coverage < 50 · ruleset rccef86aa

Thresholds are configurable policy conventions, not universal performance standards.

Only GitHub review records count. Approval given in a plain comment, over chat, or in person is invisible — a team with a different review habit will score unfairly low. Verify against the listed examples first.

83 of 90 merged pull requests carry no review record. If this team approves changes verbally or in chat, that practice is invisible here — check two or three of the named examples before treating this as a control failure.

§ 2 · Delivery

How fast does work ship — and is speed the right word?

Median time from ready to shipped

12 min ● measured · high confidence · 90 of 200 pull requests (45%)

Once a developer says “my change is ready”, this is how long it typically waits before it becomes part of the product. The slowest tenth take at least 4.1 days. A twelve-minute median beside a 7.8% review rate is not efficiency; it is an unguarded door.

Derivation

0.2 h = median( merged_at − created_at ) over 90 merged pull requests

Sample & exclusions

90 merged — counted 110 excluded (15 open — no lead time exists; 95 closed unmerged)

Assumption

A pull request counts as “ready” the moment it is opened. Teams that open drafts early to share progress will look slower here than they really are.

Exemplar

No. 1 fastapi/fastapi #16210 waited 4.2 days to merge — the slowest in scope

It is what the typical figure is built from, and removing it would move the number — the concrete case to examine first.
